Aptum · Presentation and Q&A Structure

Coles Buyer Presentation Prep Sheet

A buyer-focused preparation sheet for Justin’s Coles presentation. Use the anchors to stay on track, and the model language to practise the shape without memorising a full script.

Purpose of this sheet

This is not a script to memorise.

It is a training reference to help structure Justin’s Coles presentation using the Aptum Presentation and Q&A framework:

Frame Map Explain Signpost Respond Close

The main goal is to help the buyer follow the commercial story clearly:

Where we are now Why there is opportunity What products can unlock it What support Genki can provide What we want to align on Q&A Next step

1. Frame

Framework role: Frame
Frame the commercial purpose
Purpose

Set the commercial reason for the presentation.

The frame should make the presentation feel buyer-focused. It should not only sound like a company introduction. It should show why this matters for Coles next year.

Anchor

Coles value → next-year growth → support plan

Model version

“Today, the purpose is to show where Genki Forest can create stronger value for Coles next year — through proven brand momentum, stronger product range opportunities, and a clear support plan to drive growth.”

Watch out

Avoid making the opening too passive.

Weaker direction:

“Today I will introduce Genki Forest.”

Better direction:

“Today I’ll show how Genki Forest can create stronger value for Coles next year.”

2. Map

Framework role: Map
Map the presentation route
Purpose

Give the buyer the route before going into detail.

The map should not mention every single block. It should give the buyer three clear buckets.

Anchor

Momentum/performance → product opportunities → support/decisions

Model version

“Today I’ll cover three things: first, Genki’s brand momentum and current performance; second, the product opportunities for Coles across sparkling water, iced tea, and Alien Energy; and third, the next-year support plan and the key decisions we’d like to align on.”

Watch out

Do not turn the map into a long table of contents.

Mapping = big buckets.

Signposting = smaller moves between blocks.

3. Explain

Buyer-focused blocks

The blocks below sit inside Explain.

The aim is not to create ten equal sections. The aim is to help Justin explain the business case clearly, one block at a time.

Each block needs one job.

If one block tries to do five jobs, the presentation starts to wander.

Block 1 — Current Coles Situation / Baseline
Framework role

Explain

Purpose

Explain why this conversation matters now.

This block should come early because it grounds the presentation in Coles’ current situation, not just Genki’s brand story.

Anchor

Current range → limited visibility → next-year opportunity

Key points
  • Current Coles range is limited.
  • Only two sparkling water flavours are listed.
  • Shelf visibility is not strong enough.
  • There is a bigger opportunity if Coles expands the range and support.
Model version

“Currently, Genki Forest has two sparkling water SKUs listed in Coles. The product has potential, but the current range is still limited, and the shelf presence makes it harder for shoppers to notice the brand. So the opportunity next year is to build a stronger range, improve visibility, and support the product properly so it can perform at a higher level.”

Watch out

Do not sound like he is complaining about the current shelf position.

Frame it as an opportunity.

Useful signpost

“Let me start with where we are now with Coles, because this explains the opportunity for next year.”

Block 2 — Brand Story
Framework role

Explain

Purpose

Explain who Genki is without getting lost in ingredients.

This block should be short. It is about the brand idea, not every product detail.

Anchor

Better-for-you → strong flavour → modern brand image

Key points
  • Better-for-you beverage brand.
  • Healthier positioning without losing taste.
  • Zero sugar / lower sugar / less guilt.
  • Bold flavour compared with traditional sparkling water.
  • Brand feeling: vitality, freshness, energy.
Model version

“Genki Forest is a better-for-you beverage brand. The idea is simple: consumers want healthier drinks, but they still want strong flavour and enjoyment. Our products are designed to offer that balance — lower sugar or zero sugar options, bold taste, and a more modern brand image.”

Watch out

Do not over-explain ingredients here.

This block is about the brand promise and positioning.

Useful signpost

“Before we go into the numbers, let me briefly explain what Genki Forest stands for.”

Block 3 — Brand Momentum / Proof
Framework role

Explain

Purpose

Show this is not a small untested brand.

This block gives credibility. It should prove that Genki has already built momentum before asking Coles to support a bigger plan.

Anchor

Fast growth → global presence → proven appeal

Key points
  • Founded in 2016.
  • Exported to 40 countries.
  • Strong China sales, around AUD 1.4 billion.
  • High penetration in Chinese mainstream convenience stores and hypermarkets.
  • Strong year-on-year growth.
  • Works with major retailers globally and in Australia.
Model version

“Genki Forest was founded in 2016 and has grown very quickly. We now export to around 40 countries, including Australia, the US, Indonesia, Singapore, and Europe. In China, the brand has reached strong mainstream penetration and significant annual sales. This gives us confidence that the brand has already proven its appeal and can continue building momentum in Australia.”

Watch out

Do not spend too long on China.

China and global success should be used as proof, then brought back to Australia and Coles.

Useful signpost

“Now that we’ve covered the brand idea, the next point is momentum.”

Block 4 — Current Performance / Data
Framework role

Explain

Purpose

Pull the data into one clean proof block.

This block should make the data easier to follow. In the mock presentation, the data was useful, but scattered.

Anchor

Australia traction → retail proof → growth evidence

Key points
  • Sparkling water launched in Woolworths, Coles, Costco, and 7-Eleven.
  • Around 7 million cans sold in Australia retail channel in 2025.
  • Expected strong double-digit growth in 2026.
  • Iced tea had 53.3% year-on-year growth in 2025.
  • Iced tea has secured permanent Costco listing.
  • Strongest flavours mentioned: pomelo, pineapple, mango.
Model version

“In Australia, the brand is already building strong retail traction. Our sparkling water is now available across major channels including Woolworths, Coles, Costco, and 7-Eleven, and we sold around 7 million cans through Australian retail in 2025. For 2026, we expect strong double-digit growth. We also see strong momentum from iced tea, which achieved 53.3% year-on-year growth and has secured a permanent listing in Costco.”

Watch out

This needs to be clean and confident.

Avoid messy number correction during the presentation:

  • “something like…”
  • “probably…”
  • “sorry, maybe…”
  • “around… actually…”
Useful signpost

“From there, let’s look at what we’re already seeing in the Australian market.”

Product Opportunity Blocks — Core Pattern

Each product should follow the same pattern:

What it is → why Coles should care → next-year opportunity

This is one of Justin’s biggest upgrades.

He should not only describe the product. He needs to translate the product into Coles value.

Main anchor

Product → Coles value → next-year opportunity

Block 5 — Sparkling Water Opportunity
Framework role

Explain

Purpose

Present sparkling water as the core opportunity, not just the existing product.

Anchor

Hero product → more flavours → stronger shelf presence

Key points
  • Sparkling water is the hero product.
  • Already launched in Coles, Costco, Woolworths, and other major channels.
  • Coles currently has only two flavours.
  • Genki has a wider flavour range available.
  • More SKUs could improve shelf presence and shopper choice.
  • Possible flavour exclusivity could give Coles differentiation.
Model version

“Sparkling water is our hero product and the strongest starting point for Coles. At the moment, Coles has two flavours, but we have a wider range available. Expanding to three or more SKUs would give the brand stronger shelf presence, more flavour choice, and a clearer reason for shoppers to notice and trial the range. There may also be an opportunity to discuss exclusive flavours, which could help Coles create a more differentiated offer.”

Coles value

More SKUs = stronger shelf block, better visibility, more shopper choice, more trial, stronger category presence.

Watch out

Do not just say:

“We have six or seven flavours.”

The buyer needs to hear why more flavours matter commercially.

Useful signpost

“Let me start with sparkling water, because this is our hero product and the clearest growth opportunity for Coles.”

  • “Gen Z consumers”

  • “modern zero-sugar option”
  • “differentiated energy option”

“The third opportunity is Alien Energy, which gives us a different growth angle in a high-growth category.”

Block 8 — Commercial Translation Summary
Framework role

Explain

Purpose

Make the buyer hear the business case clearly.

This can come after the three product blocks, but the commercial translation should also appear inside each product block.

The main question is:

What does this do for Coles?

Anchor

Product facts → Coles benefit → commercial result

Key points
  • Category growth.
  • Incremental sales.
  • Better shelf presence.
  • More flavour choice.
  • Health-conscious shopper demand.
  • Asian aisle traffic plus mainstream beverage traffic.
  • Differentiation from existing beverages.
  • Potential profit/revenue return.
Model version

“Commercially, the opportunity for Coles is not only adding more products. It is about building stronger shelf presence, increasing shopper trial, creating incremental sales, and offering a differentiated better-for-you beverage range. With the right SKU selection and support plan, Genki can help Coles grow the category rather than simply compete with existing products.”

Watch out

This is where he needs to stop sounding like a product rep and start sounding like a commercial partner.

Do not only explain what the product is.

Explain what the product does for Coles.

Useful signpost

“So commercially, what does this mean for Coles?”

Block 9 — Next-Year Plan / Forecast
Framework role

Explain

Purpose

Show Coles what the growth plan depends on, without pretending Justin has perfect forecast numbers.

This block matters because Coles will likely ask:

  • What are you expecting next year?
  • What is the forecast based on?
  • Which products are priority?
  • What investment supports the forecast?
  • What return should we expect?

Justin should not panic if he does not have every exact number. He needs clear forecast logic.

Anchor

Current traction → growth drivers → product priorities → support needed → expected outcome

Core pattern

Current traction → growth drivers → product priorities → support needed → expected outcome

Key points
  • Sparkling water is the first priority because it is already established in Coles.
  • Growth depends on expanding from two flavours to a stronger range.
  • Shelf visibility matters because current presence is too limited.
  • Iced tea and Alien Energy are additional growth opportunities.
  • Forecast should connect to SKU mix, shelf position, launch timing, marketing support, and promotion plan.
  • Avoid giving exact numbers unless they are confirmed.
Model version

“For next year, our forecast depends on three main drivers: the right SKU mix, stronger shelf visibility, and a clear support plan. Our first priority is to build the sparkling water range because it is already established in Coles. Then we can look at iced tea and Alien Energy as additional growth opportunities. If we align on the right products, launch timing, and support plan, we believe Genki can deliver stronger growth for Coles next year.”

Stronger commercial version

“From a forecast point of view, the biggest growth driver is not only more products. It is the combination of the right SKUs, better visibility, and consistent support. If Coles expands the sparkling water range and we support it properly through promotion and trade marketing, the brand has a stronger chance to increase trial, repeat purchase, and overall category contribution next year.”

If the buyer asks for numbers

“I don’t want to give an unconfirmed number today, but the forecast would be based on current retail performance, the SKU range selected, shelf visibility, promotional support, and launch timing. Once we align on those points, we can come back with a more accurate forecast.”

Coles value
  • A clearer forecast helps Coles understand the business case.
  • The forecast is stronger if Coles gives the product enough space, visibility, and support.
  • Justin can frame growth as a shared plan, not just Genki hoping for sales.
Watch out

This is where he may become vague:

  • “probably”
  • “maybe”
  • “we expect good growth”
  • “something like”
  • “it depends”

Better language:

  • “Our forecast depends on…”
  • “The main growth drivers are…”
  • “The first priority is…”
  • “The strongest opportunity is…”
  • “Once we align on the range and support, we can confirm the forecast.”
Drill shape

Ask him to practise this in 45–60 seconds:

What is the forecast based on?

He should answer:

  • Current performance.
  • SKU expansion.
  • Shelf visibility.
  • Support plan.
  • Launch timing.
  • Expected growth direction.
Main coaching point

Forecast does not need to be a random number. It needs to sound like a business plan.

Useful signpost

“Looking ahead to next year, our forecast depends on three main drivers.”

Framework role

Explain moving into Close

Purpose

Make clear what Justin wants Coles to consider before Q&A starts.

This is not the final close yet.

This is the moment where he tells the buyer:

Here is what we want to align on.

Anchor

Range → visibility → support plan → launch timing

Key points
  • More sparkling water SKUs.
  • Stronger shelf visibility.
  • Possible flavour exclusivity.
  • Opportunity for iced tea.
  • Opportunity for Alien Energy.
  • Support plan for next year.
  • Launch timing.
  • What Coles would need from Genki to make this work.
Model version

“What we would like to discuss today is how Genki Forest can take a bigger role in Coles next year. The key areas are expanding the sparkling water range, improving shelf visibility, exploring the right opportunity for iced tea and Alien Energy, and aligning on the support plan needed to make the launch successful.”

Stronger commercial version

“From our side, the biggest opportunity is to build a stronger Genki range in Coles next year. That means looking at the right sparkling water SKUs, whether iced tea or Alien Energy has a role, and what level of support would give the range the best chance to perform. Watch out

This cannot be vague.

He should not say:

  • “We are happy to discuss anything.”
  • “We can do whatever you need.”
  • “Maybe we can launch more products.”
  • “Just let us know what you think.”

That sounds too passive.

Better idea:

We have a clear proposal, and we want to align it with Coles’ priorities.

“So the key question for today is how we align on the right range and support plan.”

4. Signpost

Framework role: Signpost
Signposting between blocks
Purpose

Help the buyer follow the journey between blocks.

This is not about adding more content. It is about making the movement between ideas clear.

Justin’s presentation already has a lot of information. Without signposting, it can feel like:

brand → data → product → product → product → marketing → ending

The buyer needs to feel:

I know where we are. I know why this section matters. I know where we’re going next.

Anchor

Where we are → why it matters → where we’re going next

Core rule

Use one short sentence before each new block.

Not long. Not fancy. Just clear.

Signpost into Current Coles Situation / Baseline

“Let me start with where we are now with Coles, because this explains the opportunity for next year.”

Signpost into Brand Story

“Before we go into the numbers, let me briefly explain what Genki Forest stands for.”

Signpost into Brand Momentum / Proof

“Now that we’ve covered the brand idea, the next point is momentum.”

Signpost into Current Performance / Data

“From there, let’s look at what we’re already seeing in the Australian market.”

Signpost into Product Opportunities

“Now I’d like to move into the product opportunities for Coles, starting with sparkling water.”

Signpost between Products

Sparkling Water → Iced Tea

“Moving from sparkling water, the next opportunity is iced tea, which gives Coles another proven beverage option.”

Iced Tea → Alien Energy

“The third opportunity is Alien Energy, which gives us a different growth angle in a high-growth category.”

Signpost into Commercial Translation

“So commercially, what does this mean for Coles?”

This is probably the most useful signpost for Justin.

Signpost into Next-Year Plan / Forecast

“Looking ahead to next year, our forecast depends on three main drivers.”

Signpost into Marketing Support / Investment

“To make this successful, the product plan needs to be supported properly.”

Signpost into Ask / Direction

“So the key question for today is how we align on the right range and support plan.”

Watch out

Justin should not overuse:

  • “So yeah…”
  • “And then…”
  • “Next one is…”
  • “Here is…”
  • “Okay, so…”

Those are not terrible, but they do not guide the buyer.

Better signposts do two jobs:

  • They tell the buyer where we are going.
  • They explain why that next part matters.
Main coaching point

Signposting is not decoration. It is listener control.

5. Presentation Close / Handover to Q&A

Framework role: Close before Respond
Close the presentation before Q&A
Purpose

Finish the presentation cleanly and invite buyer questions.

This is the close before Q&A.

Anchor

Main takeaway → decision areas → invite questions

Model version

“The main takeaway is that Genki Forest has strong momentum, and we believe there is a clear opportunity to build a bigger role with Coles next year. From here, the key areas to align on are: which SKUs are the best fit, what level of support Coles would need, and what launch plan gives us the strongest chance of success. I’m happy to discuss any questions.”

Simpler version for Justin

“The main takeaway is that Genki Forest is ready to play a bigger role in Coles next year. The key things to align on are the right SKUs, the support needed, and the launch plan. I’m happy to discuss any questions.”

Watch out

This should not be long.

It needs to do three things:

  • Remind them of the main message.
  • Name the decision areas.
  • Open the Q&A.

That’s it.

6. Respond — Q&A Response Control

Framework role: Respond
Q&A response control
Purpose

Help Justin answer buyer questions directly, without freezing, over-explaining, or giving control away too quickly.

This is probably the highest-pressure part of the Coles meeting.

The goal is not to give perfect answers. The goal is to stay controlled when the buyer asks fast or difficult questions.

Anchor

Direct answer → short support → commercial link → next step

Core response pattern

Direct answer → short support → commercial link → next step

That means:

  • Answer the question first.
  • Then give one reason, data point, or example.
  • Then connect it to Coles’ business.
  • Then land with what should happen next.
Why this matters for Justin

In the mock presentation, he often had the answer, but the answer came late.

The underperformance question was the clearest example. Instead of answering the backup plan first, he started with why the product should not underperform.

A stronger pattern is:

“Yes, we would have a backup plan. First, we would review performance by SKU. Then we could adjust support, review flavour mix, or discuss SKU rotation with Coles if needed.”

Then he can add confidence and data after that.

Q&A rule

Do not start with background.

Start with the answer.

Then support it.

Question Type 1 — Investment
Buyer question

“What investment are you asking from Coles, and what investment will your company put behind it?”

Anchor

Prepared to invest → level depends on range/visibility → build focused plan

Response target

Be clear that Genki will invest, but connect the level of investment to range, visibility, timing, and support plan.

Model answer

“The direct answer is that we are prepared to invest behind the brand, but the level of investment should match the range and visibility we agree on. We can support through promotion, trade marketing, social media, influencer content, and seasonal activity. If we align on the right SKUs and shelf position, we can build a more focused investment plan for next year.”

Watch out
  • Do not make vague promises.
  • Do not say “we can support anything.”
  • Do not blame the bottom shelf too strongly.
Question Type 2 — Forecast
Buyer question

“What sales forecast are you expecting next year, and what is that forecast based on?”

Anchor

Current performance → SKU selection → visibility/support/timing → accurate forecast later

Response target

Use forecast logic if exact numbers are not confirmed.

Model answer

“Our forecast would be based on five drivers: current retail performance, SKU selection, shelf visibility, promotional support, and launch timing. I don’t want to give an unconfirmed number today, but once we align on those points, we can come back with a more accurate forecast. Directionally, we believe the brand can grow strongly if the range and support plan are right.”

Watch out
  • Do not invent numbers.
  • Do not say only “we expect good growth.”
  • Explain what the forecast depends on.
Question Type 3 — Profit / Margin
Buyer question

“What profit or margin opportunity does this create for Coles?”

Anchor

Shelf presence → trial/repeat → incremental sales → supported launch

Response target

Connect profit to sales drivers, not only margin percentage.

Model answer

“The profit opportunity comes from stronger shelf presence, more shopper trial, and repeat purchase. With the right SKU mix, Genki can bring incremental sales rather than only replacing existing beverage sales. We can also support the launch with promotional and trade marketing activity, which gives the range a stronger chance to perform.”

Watch out

If he does not have exact margin data, do not fake it.

Focus on profit logic:

  • rate of sale
  • incremental sales
  • repeat purchase
  • support
Question Type 4 — Shelf Space / Visibility
Buyer question

“If we give you more space or better shelf position, what return should we expect?”

Anchor

More visibility → easier to notice → trial/repeat → return

Response target

Show why space matters commercially.

Model answer

“With more visibility, the brand has a much better chance to convert shoppers. At the moment, a limited range can make the product easy to miss. A stronger shelf block with the right flavours can increase trial, improve repeat purchase, and make the brand look more established in the category. That is where the return would come from.”

Watch out

Do not talk only about how Genki benefits.

Make it about Coles’ return.

Question Type 5 — Underperformance / Risk
Buyer question

“If the product underperforms after launch, what is the backup plan?”

Anchor

Review performance → identify issue → adjust action with Coles

Response target

Answer the backup plan first.

Model answer

“If the product underperforms, our backup plan would be to review the performance by SKU, check whether the issue is flavour, visibility, price, or support, and then agree the best action with Coles. That could mean adjusting promotion, changing the flavour mix, increasing support, or reviewing the SKU plan. We would not just list the product and leave it unsupported.”

Watch out

Do not start with:

“We don’t think that will happen.”

Confidence is fine, but the buyer asked about risk management.

Question Type 6 — Why Coles Should Support It
Buyer question

“What is the main reason Coles should support these products next year?”

Anchor

Differentiated range → proven momentum → room to grow

Response target

Give one strong commercial reason, then support.

Model answer

“The main reason is that Genki gives Coles a differentiated better-for-you beverage range with proven momentum and room to grow. Sparkling water is already established, iced tea has strong proof from Costco, and Alien Energy gives us a new growth angle. With the right range and support plan, this can help Coles grow the category and attract more shopper trial.”

Watch out

Do not give five equal reasons.

Start with the strongest reason, then group the support.

Question Type 7 — Which SKU Is Priority?
Buyer question

“Which SKU or flavour do you believe has the strongest opportunity, and why?”

Anchor

Sparkling first → strongest base → iced tea/energy as expansion

Response target

Show hierarchy, not “everything is good.”

Model answer

“Our first priority would be sparkling water, because it is already established and has the clearest base to build from. Within that, we would prioritise the flavours with the strongest market demand and shopper fit. Iced tea and Alien Energy are also strong opportunities, but sparkling water is the most natural starting point for range expansion.”

Watch out

Do not make all products sound equally important.

Buyers want prioritisation.

Question Type 8 — Support Needed from Coles
Buyer question

“What do you need from Coles to make this successful?”

Anchor

SKU range → visibility → timing → promotional support

Response target

Be clear but not demanding.

Model answer

“To make this successful, the main things we need to align on are SKU range, shelf visibility, launch timing, and promotional support. From our side, we can support with trade marketing, social media, and seasonal activity. From Coles’ side, the key is giving the range enough visibility and the right launch window to perform properly.”

Watch out

Do not say:

“We need everything.”

Name the key conditions calmly.

Recovery Phrases for Q&A

These help Justin avoid freezing.

  • “Before I answer directly, let me check one thing.”
  • “The direct answer is…”
  • “There are two parts to that.”
  • “From a Coles perspective, the key point is…”
  • “I don’t want to give an unconfirmed number today, but the forecast would be based on…”
  • “The practical next step would be to confirm…”
Main coaching point

Justin should treat Q&A like controlled mini-presentations.

Each answer needs:

  • One clear answer.
  • One short reason.
  • One Coles benefit.
  • One landing.

7. Final Close / Next Step

Framework role: Close after Respond
Close the whole meeting with action
Purpose

End the whole meeting with action, not vague politeness.

This comes after Q&A, based on what was discussed.

Anchor

Discussion → priority SKUs → support level → launch timing → final plan

Model version

“Based on today’s discussion, the next step is to confirm the priority SKUs, the support level, and the launch timing so we can build the final plan for next year.”

Stronger version

“Based on today’s discussion, I suggest we confirm three things after this meeting: which SKUs Coles wants to prioritise, what level of support is needed, and the launch timing for next year. Once we have that, we can come back with a more complete plan.”

Watch out

Do not end with:

  • “Let me know.”
  • “Hope that makes sense.”
  • “That’s everything from my side.”
  • “Any other questions?”

Those are not terrible, but they are weak as a final landing.

The final close needs:

Action + owner + next step.

Drill shape

Practise only the last 30 seconds:

Main takeaway → align on SKUs/support/timing → confirm next step

Main coaching point

A strong close does not just finish the presentation. It tells the buyer what should happen next.
