Buyer Question Recovery Lab

Use this when an Australian buyer speaks quickly, uses retail or FMCG terminology, or asks a question you only partly understand. The goal is to recover calmly, clarify the real issue, answer the right point, and close the loop with a clear next step.

Purpose of this lab

This is not a general vocabulary list.

This is a speaking practice tool for fast buyer conversations in Australian FMCG and retail.

1. Recovery Pattern

Fast buyer questions
Catch → Clarify → Confirm → Answer → Close
Catch

Listen for the key commercial word or concern.

Example: relay, rate of sale, sell-through, scanback, space-to-sales, range reset.

Clarify

Slow the moment down instead of guessing.

“Can I check what you mean by the range reset?”

Confirm

Repeat the issue back in cleaner language.

“So the concern is whether the product justifies the space.”

Answer

Give the direct answer first.

Then add one reason, data point, or commercial link.

Close

Confirm action, owner, and timeline.

“We’ll come back by Friday with revised numbers.”

Model recovery

“Let me make sure I’ve understood. Your concern is whether the product can justify the shelf space based on current rate of sale. Is that right?”

“Okay, that makes sense. We’ll review the sell-through and come back with a clearer commercial case by Friday.”

Watch out

Do not half-understand the question and then answer the wrong thing confidently.

In buyer meetings, clarification is not weakness. It is control.

2. Clarification Phrase Bank

Recovery language
When you only catch part of the question
Partial understanding

“I caught the main point, but can I check one detail?”

Fast speech

“Could you rephrase that last part?”

Unclear concern

“Are you mainly concerned about the forecast, the support plan, or the shelf space?”

Unknown retail term

“Sorry, I missed that term — could you say it again?”

Before answering

“Before I answer directly, let me check I’m answering the right point.”

Commercial clarification

“Is the main issue performance, space, or timing?”

Anchor

Pause → check → answer the real question

3. Close the Loop

Action + timeline
Request → action → owner → timeline
Purpose

When the buyer gives a request, requirement, or concern, the meeting should not end with: “Okay, we’ll check.”

Close the loop by confirming what happens next, who owns it, and when it is due.

Anchor

Request → action → owner → timeline

Weak

“Okay, we’ll check.”

Stronger

“Okay, we’ll review the sell-through and come back by Friday with a clearer commercial case.”

Timeline

“When would you need this finalised by?”

Deliverable

“What would you like us to come back with — revised numbers, launch timing, or support options?”

Commitment

“We’ll come back to you by Friday with the revised forecast and support plan.”

5. Australian Buyer Language

Soft slang + natural phrases
Common phrases you may hear
“A bit light-on”

Not strong enough, not detailed enough, or missing support.

“The support plan feels a bit light-on at the moment.”

“Not quite there yet”

Not ready or not convincing enough yet.

“The commercial case is not quite there yet.”

“Worth a look”

Potentially interesting, but not a commitment.

“The mango flavour could be worth a look.”

“Flat out”

Very busy.

“We’re flat out with range review at the moment.”

“Heaps of”

A lot of. Common in casual Australian English.

“There’s heaps of pressure on shelf space.”

“Let’s park that”

Leave that topic for later.

“Let’s park that for now and come back to the support plan.”

“Too easy”

No problem / that’s fine. Casual, friendly Australian response.

“Too easy, we can send that through after the meeting.”

“No dramas”

No problem. Casual and relaxed.

“No dramas, we’ll update the forecast and send it through.”

Watch out

Use casual phrases lightly. In buyer meetings, keep the main message professional and use casual language only for rapport or soft transitions.

7. Practice Routine

Use in class or solo practice
15-minute drill format
Step 1

Choose one buyer comment.

Read it quickly once.

Step 2

Catch the keywords and clarify.

No full answer yet.

Step 3

Answer the point and close the loop with action + timeline.

Success condition

You do not need perfect English. You need control:

“Let me make sure I’ve understood…” → clarify → answer → action/timeline.

Solo practice method
Method

Pick one term and one drill. Read the buyer sentence once. Close the card. Then respond out loud.

Your target

Do not memorise the whole answer. Memorise the recovery move.

Practice reminder

“I don’t need to understand every word. I need to catch the key point, check the meaning, and land the next step.”
