Keeping the Thread in Fast Meetings

A personalised meeting-control lesson for Onur. The goal is not to catch every word. The goal is to stay oriented, recover quickly, and participate clearly in international internal meetings.

Why this lesson exists

Onur’s current English use is mainly inside a global company: internal meetings, international colleague communication, product/system discussions, presentations, customer-support conversations, follow-ups, and cross-cultural teamwork.

The key pressure pattern is not simply “I need more vocabulary.” It is:

“I understand sentences, but I cannot combine all the sentences in my mind. When the meeting finishes, I think: what happened in the meeting?”

This lesson trains the missing bridge between listening and speaking:

Stay oriented Track the topic Catch the main point Identify the meeting status Recover the connection Confirm the action

1. The Real Problem

Before participation comes orientation
The trap: listening word by word
What happens in fast meetings

In a fast English meeting, it is easy to listen too closely to individual words. The problem is that while you are still processing one sentence, the meeting has already moved to the next point.

By the end, you may remember words, phrases, and pieces of information, but not the full direction of the meeting.

Weak strategy

Trying to understand every word before participating.

This creates pressure, slows processing, and makes it harder to follow the whole discussion.

Stronger strategy

Track what the meeting is doing.

Ask yourself: What are we discussing now? What is the main point? Is this an update, problem, decision, risk, question, or action? What needs to happen next?

The coaching target for Onur
Main focus

The main focus is keeping the thread in fast internal meetings.

Other meeting skills matter too — entering, contributing, challenging, and summarising — but they become much easier after Onur can stay oriented in the conversation.

Lesson direction

First stay connected. Then clarify. Then contribute.

Work situations this connects to
  • Q2 evaluation meetings.
  • Meetings with international colleagues, including colleagues in France.
  • Product group discussions.
  • Library system and sensory test system explanations.
  • Customer-support discussions with the sales team.
  • Product samples, trials, technical communication, and follow-ups.
  • Future presentations about what he has done and what needs to happen next.

2. The T-P-S-A Map

Topic · Point · Status · Action
The four things to track
T
Topic

What are we discussing now?

Example: Q2 results, customer trial, product sample, sensory test system.

P
Point

What is the main idea?

Example: The customer needs more technical information before continuing.

S
Status

What kind of discussion is this?

Update, problem, decision, risk, question, action, or background information.

A
Action

What needs to happen next?

Example: Confirm the timeline, check product details, follow up with sales.

Core idea

If you can identify the Topic, Point, Status, and Action, you are still inside the meeting.

3. Recovery Language

When the thread becomes unclear
Reset the topic
Purpose

Use this when the discussion changes direction and you are not sure what topic the group is on.

Model language

“Just to make sure I’m following, are we talking about the customer trial or the internal timeline?”

“Are we still discussing the Q2 results, or have we moved to the next project?”

“Can I quickly check the topic here? Is this about the library system or the sensory test system?”

Avoid

“I don’t understand.”

This is too general. It does not show which part is unclear.

Check the main point
Purpose

Use this when you understood the words, but you are not sure about the message.

Model language

“So the main point is that the customer needs more technical information, right?”

“So the key issue is the approval step, not the product itself. Is that correct?”

“So we are saying the trial went well, but the next step is still unclear?”

Identify the status
Purpose

Use this when you are not sure whether the group is only sharing information or actually making a decision.

Model language

“Are we discussing this as an update, or do we need to make a decision today?”

“Is this something we need to solve now, or is it just for our information?”

“Is this a risk we need to manage, or just a normal delay?”

Why this matters

Technical and sales-support meetings often mix updates, problems, risks, and actions. If you identify the status, the meeting becomes easier to follow.

Recover the connection
Purpose

Use this when you understood two separate points, but not how they connect.

Model language

“I missed the connection between those two points. Is the delay related to the approval process?”

“Can I check how this connects to the customer trial?”

“Sorry, I understood the product issue, but I missed how it affects the timeline.”

Main phrase for Onur

“I missed the connection between those two points.”

This directly matches the real problem: understanding sentences, but not connecting the full discussion.

Confirm the action
Purpose

Use this when the meeting is about to move on, but the responsibility or timing is unclear.

Model language

“Before we move on, can we confirm the action point?”

“So the next step is for me to check the product details and send an update, correct?”

“Who will follow up with the customer after this meeting?”

“Can we confirm the owner and timing for this action?”

Strong student response

“Just to make sure I’m following, the customer feedback was positive, but before sending new samples, we need to confirm the correct product version with the technical team. Is that right?”

Practice 2 — Q2 evaluation and project delay
Read aloud

“The sales numbers for Q2 were strong overall, especially in some regions. But for the dairy projects, we had some delays because the approval process took longer than expected. That may affect the timing for the next customer trials.”

Task

Ask Onur to map T-P-S-A, then ask one recovery question.

Strong recovery question

“I missed the connection between the approval process and the next customer trials. Is the delay affecting when we can start the trials?”

Alternative answer

“Can I quickly check my understanding? The sales results were strong, but some dairy projects are delayed because of approval timing. Is that correct?”

Practice 3 — Sudden opinion question
Read aloud

“Onur, what do you think? Should we send the samples this week or wait until we confirm the application details?”

Task

Buy time, then answer with one clear point and one reason.

Strong answer

“Let me take a second to think about that. My first thought is that we should confirm the application details first, because if we send the wrong sample or incomplete information, it may create confusion for the customer.”

Shorter answer

“I think we should confirm the application details first. Then we can send the samples with the correct explanation.”

Practice 4 — Meeting going in circles
Read aloud

“We need to move quickly.”
“Yes, but we don’t have the full technical details.”
“The customer is waiting.”
“Exactly, so maybe we should send something today.”
“But if we send it too early, we may need to correct it later.”

Task

Summarise the situation and move the meeting forward.

Strong answer

“It sounds like we agree on the goal: we need to respond to the customer quickly. But the open point is whether the technical details are ready. Before we move on, can we confirm who will check the details and when we can reply?”

Practice 5 — International colleague meet and greet
Read aloud

“Hi Onur, it’s nice to finally meet you. I’m based in France and I mainly support this product group from the technical side. If you have questions about customer applications or product version details, I can usually help, but for pricing or commercial decisions, it’s better to check with the sales manager.”

Task

Track the T-P-S-A, then ask one useful follow-up question.

Strong follow-up

“Great, thank you. Just to make sure I understand, I should contact you for technical application questions, but check with the sales manager for pricing or commercial decisions. Is that right?”

Useful relationship-building question

“Could you tell me the best way to contact you if I have a technical question after a customer meeting?”

Practice 6 — Sensory test system training
Read aloud

“For the sensory test system, the main thing is to enter the correct sample information before the test starts. If the sample code or product version is wrong, the results may not be useful later. So before using the system with a real customer project, please check the sample details carefully.”

Task

Ask Onur to identify the warning and action.

Strong answer

“So the main point is that the sample information must be correct before the test starts. If the sample code or product version is wrong, the results may not be useful. The action is to check the sample details carefully before using the system for a real customer project.”
