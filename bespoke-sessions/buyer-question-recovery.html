Buyer Question Recovery Lab

A practice lab for fast buyer questions, FMCG terminology, clarification, and closing the loop. Use it when Justin catches only part of a buyer’s question and needs to recover without guessing.

Purpose of this lab

This is not a vocabulary dictionary.

It is a speaking practice tool for the moment when a buyer speaks quickly, uses commercial terminology, or gives a requirement that needs a clear follow-up.

Catch Clarify Confirm Answer Close the Loop

Main training goal:

Catch the keyword Clarify the issue Confirm the meaning Answer directly Action + owner + timeline

1. How to Use This Lab

Practice control panel
Lesson routine
Core practice pattern

Buyer comment → Justin recovers → coach corrects one thing → Justin repeats cleaner

Round 1

Choose one fast buyer comment.

Justin responds using the recovery pattern.

Round 2

Add one FMCG term.

Justin clarifies, answers, and closes the loop.

Coach rule

Do not correct everything. Correct one thing per round: clarification, direct answer, timeline, or commercial wording.

2. Recovery Pattern

Fast buyer questions
Catch → Clarify → Confirm → Answer → Close
Catch

Listen for the key commercial word or concern.

Example: forecast gap, USPW, rate of sale, support plan, incremental sales.

Clarify

Slow the moment down instead of guessing.

“Can I check what you mean by the gap?”

Confirm

Repeat the issue back in cleaner language.

“So your concern is whether the forecast is realistic.”

Answer

Give the direct answer first.

Then add one reason or data point.

Close

Confirm action, owner, and timeline.

“We’ll come back by Friday with revised numbers.”

Model recovery

“Let me make sure I’ve understood. Your concern is whether the forecast is realistic compared with the current rate of sale. Is that right?”

“Okay, that makes sense. We’ll review the numbers and come back with a revised forecast by Friday.”

Watch out

Do not half-understand the question and then answer the wrong thing confidently.

In buyer meetings, clarification is not weakness. It is control.

3. Clarification Phrase Bank

Recovery language
When Justin only catches part of the question
Partial understanding

“I caught the main point, but can I check one detail?”

Fast speech

“Could you rephrase that last part?”

Unclear concern

“Are you mainly concerned about the forecast, the support plan, or the SKU selection?”

Forecast gap

“Let me make sure I’ve understood the gap.”

Specific word missed

“Sorry, I missed that one term — could you say it again?”

Before answering

“Before I answer directly, let me check I’m answering the right point.”

Anchor

Pause → check → answer the real question

4. Close the Loop

Boss feedback
Action + owner + timeline
Purpose

When the buyer gives a request, requirement, or concern, the meeting should not end with: “Okay, we’ll check.”

Justin needs to confirm what happens next, who owns it, and when it is due.

Anchor

Request → action → owner → timeline

Weak

“Okay, we’ll check.”

Stronger

“Okay, we’ll review the forecast and come back by Friday with a revised number and support plan.”

Timeline

“When would you need this finalised by?”

Deliverable

“What would you like us to come back with — revised numbers, launch timing, or support options?”

Commitment

“We’ll come back to you by Friday with the revised forecast and support plan.”

5. FMCG Term Builder

Mnemonic + chunk + use
How to learn a term
Learning cycle

Meaning → phrase/chunk → mnemonic → Genki sentence → buyer-response sentence

Watch out

Do not learn every possible meaning first.

Start with the meaning Justin is most likely to hear or use in FMCG buyer conversations.

Range rationalisation
Meaning

Cutting SKUs from the range to simplify the shelf, improve performance, or make space for stronger products.

Chunk

risk of range rationalisation

Mnemonic hook

A rational buyer with scissors cuts weak SKUs from the shelf.

Genki sentence

“If Coles is going through range rationalisation, every SKU needs a clear commercial reason to stay.”

Buyer-response sentence

“I understand. If range rationalisation is a focus, we need to show which SKUs can deliver incremental value.”

Rate of sale
Meaning

How quickly a product sells in store.

Chunk

current rate of sale

Mnemonic hook

A cash register racing down the shelf because the product sells fast.

Genki sentence

“The current rate of sale is still early, but it gives us a starting point for the forecast.”

Buyer-response sentence

“Are you concerned that the proposed USPW is too high compared with the current rate of sale?”

Sell-through
Meaning

How much product actually sells through to shoppers, not just how much is shipped into the retailer.

Chunk

strong sell-through

Mnemonic hook

A can travelling through the shelf, through the checkout, and into the shopper’s bag.

Genki sentence

“We need to review sell-through before we overcommit to a wider rollout.”

Buyer-response sentence

“That makes sense. We can come back with sell-through data and a clearer view of repeat purchase.”

Incremental sales
Meaning

Extra sales that would not have happened without the product, range expansion, promotion, or support.

Chunk

create incremental sales

Mnemonic hook

A shelf grows an extra step upward: that extra step is incremental sales.

Genki sentence

“The goal is to create incremental sales, not simply move sales from one SKU to another.”

Buyer-response sentence

“Your question is whether the extra SKUs would create incremental sales or just add shelf pressure. Is that right?”

Cannibalisation
Meaning

When one product takes sales from another product in the same range instead of creating new growth.

Chunk

risk of cannibalisation

Mnemonic hook

One Genki can eats another Genki can’s sales.

Genki sentence

“We need to choose flavours carefully to reduce the risk of cannibalisation.”

Buyer-response sentence

“I understand the concern. The key is choosing SKUs that grow the range rather than cannibalise the existing flavours.”

Commercial case
Meaning

The business reason for listing, supporting, expanding, or keeping a product.

Chunk

build a stronger commercial case

Mnemonic hook

A briefcase full of sales data, margin, support plans, and buyer reasons.

Genki sentence

“We need a stronger commercial case for each SKU before asking for more space.”

Buyer-response sentence

“That’s fair. We can build a clearer commercial case around rate of sale, support, and expected return.”

6. Fast Buyer Comment Drills

Core practice
Drill 1 — Forecast gap
Buyer says

“The USPW looks higher than what we’re seeing in the current rate of sale.”

Catch

USPW → current rate of sale → forecast gap

Strong recovery response

“Let me make sure I’ve understood. Are you saying the proposed USPW feels too high compared with the current rate of sale?”

“That makes sense. We can review the forecast and come back with a revised number by Friday.”

Coach push

Make Justin clarify the gap before defending the forecast.

Drill 2 — Incremental sales vs shelf pressure
Buyer says

“If we add more SKUs, we need to understand whether this creates incremental sales or just puts more pressure on shelf space.”

Catch

more SKUs → incremental sales → shelf pressure

Strong recovery response

“Let me check I’ve understood. Your concern is whether the extra SKUs would grow the category, or simply add pressure to limited shelf space. Is that right?”

“The way we would address that is by prioritising the SKUs with the clearest shopper fit and strongest support plan.”

Coach push

Do not let him say only “we have many flavours.” Push the commercial meaning.

Drill 3 — Sell-through proof
Buyer says

“Before we commit to support, we need a clearer view of sell-through and repeat purchase.”

Catch

support → sell-through → repeat purchase

Strong recovery response

“Understood. You need more confidence that shoppers are not only trying the product, but buying it again.”

“We’ll review the latest sell-through and come back with a clearer read on repeat purchase and support options.”

Coach push

Make him paraphrase the buyer’s concern before promising action.

Drill 4 — Range rationalisation risk
Buyer says

“We’re going through range rationalisation, so any additional SKU needs a very clear reason to be there.”

Catch

range rationalisation → fewer SKUs → clear commercial reason

Strong recovery response

“That makes sense. If Coles is rationalising the range, every SKU needs to justify its place.”

“Our focus would be to show which Genki SKUs bring the clearest incremental value, shopper fit, and support plan.”

Coach push

Make him avoid sounding defensive. Acknowledge the commercial logic first.

Drill 5 — Need a timeline
Buyer says

“We’ll need more clarity on the support plan before we can take this further.”

Catch

support plan → more clarity → next step

Strong recovery response

“Absolutely. What would you like us to come back with — trade support, launch timing, or the full support calendar?”

“And when would you need that finalised by?”

Coach push

This is the boss-feedback drill. Do not let the response end without a timeline.

Drill 6 — Buyer speaks too fast
Buyer says

“The support looks okay directionally, but if the forecast is built off assumptions rather than actual sell-through, it’s harder for us to justify more space.”

Catch

support okay → forecast assumptions → sell-through → justify space

Strong recovery response

“I caught the main point, but can I check one detail?”

“Is your main concern that the forecast needs stronger sell-through evidence before Coles can justify more space?”

Coach push

Train him to recover calmly instead of panicking when he misses part of the sentence.

7. Practice Routine

Use in class
15-minute drill format
Step 1

Choose one drill card.

Read the buyer line quickly.

Step 2

Justin catches keywords and clarifies.

No full answer yet.

Step 3

Justin answers and closes the loop with action + timeline.

Success condition

Justin does not need perfect English. He needs control:

“Let me make sure I’ve understood…” → clarify → answer → action/timeline.

Coach Notes

Private lesson guidance
How to coach this without overbuilding
Main focus

This is not a theory lesson. It is a pressure-recovery drill.

Best correction type

Correct the recovery move, not every grammar issue.

Examples: clarify earlier, paraphrase cleaner, close the loop, add timeline.

Avoid
  • Turning the lesson into a general FMCG dictionary.
  • Explaining memory theory for too long.
  • Letting Justin answer before clarifying.
  • Letting “we’ll check” pass as a final answer.
Main coaching point

Buyer recovery is not about understanding every word. It is about catching enough, checking the meaning, and landing the next step.
